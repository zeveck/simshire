# Sim Shire — brief

**In one sentence:** a cozy grid-based settlement builder where you lay out
homes, farms, orchards, bridges and gathering places so that ever more
different fantasy folk — halflings, dwarves, elves, trolls, goblins, and at
last a dragon — can live side by side in peace.

**The feeling:** warm, unhurried, storybook. A pipe-smoke-and-second-breakfast
kind of chill. The tension is gentle and puzzle-like ("the elves can't stand the
smithy's clatter, but the dwarves need it"), never punishing. Success feels like
a village humming with little contented folk; the finale is a lantern-lit party
with fireworks where everyone, dragon included, celebrates together.

**Who and where:** casual and family players, on desktop (mouse) or phone
(touch, landscape). Sessions of 5–10 minutes per level, roughly 30–40 minutes
for the whole game. No reflexes needed, no fail-out game over.

## Style references

- **SimCity (classic):** borrow zoning-as-puzzle, the satisfaction of a growing
  population counter, residents moving in over time, an at-a-glance happiness
  overlay. Avoid: its UI, icons, names, city-scale scope, disasters.
- **Civilization:** borrow the "one more turn" sense of steady unlocks and the
  idea that terrain (river, forest, hills) shapes what you build where. Avoid:
  war, tech trees, its art and names.
- **The Hobbit / pastoral fantasy in general:** borrow the mood — round doors
  set into green hills, orchards, inns, stone bridges, lanterns, fireworks at a
  party. Avoid: Tolkien names and trademarks ("hobbit", "the Shire" as a place,
  named characters, specific locations, Gandalf-style wizard fireworks). Our
  folk are generic fantasy **halflings**; the title "Sim Shire" uses "shire" in
  its plain English sense (a country district) and is the person's chosen name.
- Cozy builders (Townscaper-ish calm, Stardew-ish warmth) for mood only.

## Settings and decisions made for the player

- Platform both, target web, scope medium, budget $20, 2 review rounds.
- Landscape on phones (a town map reads best wide); portrait shows a
  "turn your phone" note.
- Five levels, each adding a new folk; the fifth adds the dragon and ends with
  the party.
- No harsh losing: unhappy households move out instead of fighting; the player
  can always fix things and recover.
- Original races and building names only; nothing taken from any game or book.
